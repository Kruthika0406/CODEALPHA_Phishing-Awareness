What is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of cyberattack where attackers pretend to be a trusted person, company, or organization to trick people into revealing sensitive information.

Attackers may try to steal passwords, banking information, account credentials, or other personal information.

📧

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to trick users into clicking links or sharing information.

📱

Smishing

Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or messaging applications.

📞

Vishing

Fraudulent phone calls used to obtain sensitive information.

🌐

Website Phishing

Fake websites designed to look like legitimate websites.

How to Recognize Phishing Emails

📧 Suspicious Email Example

From: security-alert@example-security.com

Subject: URGENT! Your account will be suspended

Hello Customer,

We detected unusual activity on your account. Your account will be suspended within 24 hours.

Click the link below immediately to verify your account.

Thank you,
Security Team

⚠️ This is a simulated phishing example. Never click suspicious links without verifying the sender and destination.

Warning Signs in Phishing Emails

🚨

Urgent Language

Attackers may create fear or urgency to make you act without thinking.

🔗

Suspicious Links

Links may lead to websites that are unrelated to the organization they claim to represent.

👤

Unknown Sender

Check the sender's address carefully.

📝

Unexpected Attachments

Unexpected attachments can be dangerous.

🔐

Password Requests

Legitimate organizations generally should not ask for your password through email.

❌

Spelling Errors

Poor grammar or unusual wording can be a warning sign, although professional-looking scams also exist.

How to Identify Fake Websites

Fake websites often imitate trusted services. Check the website carefully before entering sensitive information.

🌐 suspicious-example.com

⚠️ Suspicious Website

  • ❌ Strange domain name
  • ❌ Unexpected login request
  • ❌ Poor or unusual design
  • ❌ Pressure to act immediately
  • ❌ Suspicious links
🔒 Official website

✅ Safer Website Practices

  • ✅ Check the domain carefully
  • ✅ Use bookmarks for important websites
  • ✅ Verify the website independently
  • ✅ Look for HTTPS, but don't rely on HTTPS alone
  • ✅ Avoid links from unexpected messages

💡 Website Safety Tip

Instead of clicking a login link in an unexpected email, open your browser and navigate to the organization's official website yourself.

Social Engineering Tactics

Social engineering manipulates people rather than directly attacking computer systems.

⏰

Urgency

"Act immediately or your account will be closed."

😨

Fear

Attackers may use threats or frightening messages to pressure victims.

🎁

Reward

Fake prizes, offers, or rewards may be used to attract victims.

👔

Impersonation

Attackers may pretend to be managers, banks, delivery services, or technical support.

Real-World Phishing Examples

📧 Email Account Scam

Attackers send messages claiming that an email account has a security problem and ask the user to sign in through a provided link.

Lesson: Verify unexpected security messages independently.

🏦 Banking Scam

A message may claim that a bank account has been locked and ask the recipient to confirm account details through a link.

Lesson: Contact your bank using an official phone number or website rather than the information in the message.

📦 Delivery Scam

A fake delivery notification may claim that a package cannot be delivered and request payment or personal information.

Lesson: Check delivery information through the courier's official website or application.

How to Stay Safe

1

Think Before You Click

Do not click unexpected links or attachments.

2

Verify the Sender

Check the sender's address carefully.

3

Use Multi-Factor Authentication

MFA provides an additional layer of account protection.

4

Use Strong Passwords

Use unique passwords for different accounts.

5

Keep Software Updated

Install security and software updates regularly.

6

Report Suspicious Messages

Report suspected phishing messages to your organization or service provider.
